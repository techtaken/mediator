# Understanding a Dispute Between Two People

At its simplest, a dispute is when **two parties want things that seem incompatible, and at least one of them cares enough to push for it.** That's true whether the fight is about ₹5,000, a piece of land, or who apologizes first.

Below is a way to turn something as messy as "an argument" into a clear structure.

---

## 1. The basic parts of any dispute

Every dispute, big or small, has the same parts:

| Part | Question it answers | Example (money) | Example (ego) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Parties** | Who is involved? | A and B (friends) | Two siblings |
| **Trigger** | What set it off? | B didn't repay on time | A was mocked at dinner |
| **Subject / stake** | What is being fought over? | ₹50,000 loan | Respect, being "right" |
| **Position** | What each side *says* they want | A: "Pay me back now." B: "I'll pay next month." | "Say sorry." / "I did nothing wrong." |
| **Interest** | What each side *actually* needs underneath | A: security, trust. B: time, not feeling shamed | Feeling valued, not losing face |
| **Perception** | How each side tells the story | A: "He's dishonest." B: "He's being harsh." | Each sees themselves as the victim |
| **Emotion / intensity** | How heated it is | Annoyed, then angry | Hurt, then cold |
| **Power balance** | Who has more leverage? | A holds the debt claim | Elder vs younger sibling |
| **History** | Earlier conflicts or a pattern | B was late before too | Old rivalry |
| **Relationship value** | How much both care about staying connected | Long friendship | Family, can't fully walk away |
| **Status** | Where it stands now | Escalating, Paused, Resolved… | |
| **Resolution** | How it ended, if it did | Payment plan agreed | Mutual apology |

> **Key idea:** **Positions** are what people argue about. **Interests** are what the dispute is really about. Most disputes look like money or land on the surface but run on interests like respect, fairness, security or identity.

---

## 2. Can all disputes be categorized?

Yes. A well-known framework is Christopher Moore's *Circle of Conflict*, which groups disputes by their **root cause**:

| Category | Root cause | Everyday examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Interest** | Competing over limited resources | Money, land, inheritance, a shared car |
| **Data / facts** | Different information or readings of it | "You said ₹10k," "No, I said ₹15k" |
| **Relationship / ego** | Feelings, miscommunication, pride, past hurt | Insults, feeling disrespected, jealousy |
| **Values** | Different beliefs about right and wrong | Religion, politics, how to raise kids |
| **Structural** | The situation itself creates the clash | Shared boundary wall, roommates with one bathroom, boss and employee |

**Important:** real disputes are almost always a **mix**. A fight about money (interest) often turns into a fight about ego (relationship) once someone says "You always do this."

---

## 3. One object or many?

**Short answer:** treat a dispute as **one container** that holds **several related parts.** A single flat object isn't enough, because:

- One argument usually contains **several issues**. "The loan" can turn into "the loan + the insult + who told our parents."
- Each person has their **own** position, interests and version of events, so each side needs its own record.
- A dispute **changes over time**: it escalates, cools down, flares up again.
- The **cause can shift**. It starts as money and ends as ego.

### A simple model

```
DISPUTE (the container)
├── Parties
│   ├── Party A → position, interests, emotions, story
│   └── Party B → position, interests, emotions, story
├── Issues (one or more)
│   ├── Issue 1: ₹50,000 repayment     [category: Interest]
│   ├── Issue 2: "You called me a cheat" [category: Ego/Relationship]
│   └── Issue 3: Disagreement on amount  [category: Data]
├── Context
│   ├── Relationship type & value
│   ├── Power balance
│   └── History
├── Events (timeline)
│   ├── Trigger → argument → silence → third party joins → …
└── Resolution
   ├── Method (talk, compromise, mediation, court, avoidance)
   └── Outcome (resolved / partly resolved / dormant)
```

Or as a structured object:

## 5. Ways a dispute can end

Based on the Thomas–Kilmann model, each person tends to handle conflict in one of these ways:

| Style | What it looks like | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **Competing** | "I win, you lose" | Short-term win, damaged relationship |
| **Avoiding** | Ignoring it, going silent | The dispute stays alive underneath |
| **Accommodating** | Giving in to keep the peace | Peace now, resentment later |
| **Compromising** | Meeting halfway | Both partly satisfied |
| **Collaborating** | Working on the underlying **interests** | Best long-term outcome |

---

## The short version

1. **A dispute = parties + issues + positions + interests + context + time.**
2. **The category depends on the root cause** (interest, data, relationship/ego, values, structural), and most disputes are a mix.
3. **Model it as one container with many parts**, not one flat record, because issues multiply and change.
4. **The surface topic (money, land) is rarely the whole story.** To understand a dispute, look for what each side needs underneath: respect, fairness, security or identity.

# When Each Party Has a Different Issue

You're right, and the model I gave you missed this. It treated "the issues" as one shared list, as if both people agreed on what the fight is about. Often they don't. **Two people can be in the same dispute while fighting about completely different things.**

---

## 1. Example

**Situation:** A lent B ₹50,000. B paid late. A brought it up loudly at a family dinner.

| | Party A | Party B |
|---|---|---|
| **What A thinks the dispute is** | "B owes me money and is unreliable." | — |
| **What B thinks the dispute is** | — | "A humiliated me in front of family." |
| **Category** | Interest (money) | Relationship / ego |
| **What would end it for them** | Getting repaid | Getting an apology |

If B repays, A's issue is solved. B's issue is still open. If A apologizes, B is satisfied, but A is still waiting for the money. **Each person's fix leaves the other person's problem untouched.** That's why these disputes drag on: the two sides are talking past each other.

---

## 2. How the two sides' issues can differ

| Type of mismatch | What it looks like | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Different issue** | Each person is fighting about something else | Money vs. humiliation |
| **Same issue, different category** | Same topic, different reason it matters | Land: A sees **value** (interest), B sees **ancestral pride** (values/ego) |
| **Same issues, different priority** | Both see both issues but rank them differently | A: money first, apology later. B: apology first, money is minor |
| **Different facts** | They disagree about what happened | A: "I said it once, quietly." B: "You announced it to everyone." |
| **One-sided dispute** | One person doesn't think there's a dispute at all | B is hurt and goes silent; A has no idea anything is wrong |
| **Hidden issue** | The stated issue covers the real one | "It's about the money" really means "You always favored your brother" |

---

## 3. Fixing the model: issues belong to each party

Instead of one shared issue list, **each party keeps its own list of issues as they see them.** An issue counts as "shared" only when both people acknowledge it.

```
DISPUTE
├── Party A
│   ├── Perceived issues: [Unpaid loan, Unreliability]
│   ├── Story / facts as A sees them
│   └── What A needs to feel it's resolved
├── Party B
│   ├── Perceived issues: [Public humiliation]
│   ├── Story / facts as B sees them
│   └── What B needs to feel it's resolved
├── Issue links          ← how the two sides' issues connect
│   └── "Unpaid loan" ──caused──▶ "Public humiliation"
└── Shared ground        ← issues both acknowledge (may be empty!)
```

### As a structured object

```json
{
 "dispute_id": "D-001",
 "parties": {
   "A": {
     "perceived_issues": ["I1", "I2"],
     "priority": ["I1", "I2"],
     "resolution_needs": ["Full repayment"],
     "aware_of_dispute": true
   },
   "B": {
     "perceived_issues": ["I3"],
     "priority": ["I3"],
     "resolution_needs": ["Private apology"],
     "aware_of_dispute": true
   }
 },
 "issues": [
   {"id": "I1", "topic": "Unpaid ₹50,000", "category": "Interest", "raised_by": "A", "acknowledged_by": ["A", "B"]},
   {"id": "I2", "topic": "B is unreliable", "category": "Relationship", "raised_by": "A", "acknowledged_by": ["A"]},
   {"id": "I3", "topic": "Humiliated at dinner", "category": "Ego", "raised_by": "B", "acknowledged_by": ["B"]}
 ],
 "issue_links": [
   {"from": "I1", "to": "I3", "type": "caused"}
 ],
 "shared_issues": ["I1"],
 "status": "Escalating"
}
```

The fields that make this work:

- **`raised_by`**: whose issue it is.
- **`acknowledged_by`**: whether the other side even accepts it as an issue.
- **`issue_links`**: how one person's issue grew out of the other's (caused, triggered, made worse, used as an excuse).
- **`resolution_needs` for each party**: the dispute is only fully resolved when **both** lists are satisfied.

---

## 4. What this means for resolving it

1. **Ask each side: "What is this about for you?"** Don't assume the topic.
2. **Map both lists side by side.** Look for where they overlap, where they're linked and where they're completely separate.
3. **Address each person's main issue.** Solving only one side's issue leaves half the dispute alive.
4. **Use the links.** If A's money issue caused B's ego issue, one package can close both: "I'll repay by the 15th" plus "I'm sorry I raised it in front of everyone."

---

## The short version

> A dispute isn't one disagreement. It's **two (or more) sets of perceived issues that collide.** Model the issues **per person**, track **which ones the other side acknowledges**, and **link** how they connect. The dispute is resolved only when **each person's own issue** is resolved, not just "the" issue.
